In WHIRL, these Web information sources are modeled as relations in the natural way. (You might want step through the example pages from the superkid and publisher relations to get the idea. Notice the eyes, which indicate parts of the demo that you can actually follow in this canned version.)

The key idea in WHIRL is to do "soft matching" on names. Instead of joining relations by looking for pairs of tuples with identical keys, WHIRL joins by finding pairs of tuples with "similar names". The answer to a join is a ranked list of pairings, ordered by (in this case) the similarity of the names being used as keys.

The table in the middle of the accompanying slide shows a traditional a "hard" join on the left. On the right is the "soft", similarity-based, version of the join that's supported by WHIRL. If you hit the "submit" button, you'll be able to page through WHIRL's output. WHIRL starts with matches that it is very confident in, and then, as the user requests more data, provides gradually more and more tenuous matches. The "Scholastic/Microsoft" and "Microsoft Kids" pairing appears toward the bottom of the second screen. Note that this pairing is after several incorrect matches, which is unfortunate; this means that there's no simple threshold for the degree of similarity that catches all the "good" pairings and none of the "bad" ones. We'll get back to this point later.

More generally, a WHERE clause for WHIRL can be a conjunction of "hard" conditions (like equality, etc) and "soft" conditions (like similarity of pairs of names). As noted in the slide, similarity is based on very general heuristics for matching textual items developed in information retrieval; if you're interested in the details of how I've made these work (and work efficiently) you should look at my long paper in SIGMOD-98.
