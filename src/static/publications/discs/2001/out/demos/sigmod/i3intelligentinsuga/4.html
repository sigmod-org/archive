The user invokes the DIFF operator by selecting the two cells whose difference he wants to analyze further. The output returned is shown in the screen underneath.

The output is a list of at most 10 rows (the number 10 is configurable by user) that concisely explain the reasons for the drop from 1990 to 1991. The first row shows that after discounting the rows explicitly mentioned below it, the overall revenue actually increased by 10\%. The next four rows (rows second through fifth) identify entries that were largely responsible for the large drop. For instance, for ``Operating systems'' on ``Multiuser Mainframe IBM'' the revenue decreased from 97 to 1.5, a factor of 60 reduction and for ``Operating systems'' and all platforms of type ``Wn16'' the total revenue dropped from 94.3 to 10.7. The last three rows show cases where the revenue showed significantly more than the 10\% overall increase indicated by the first row.

Thus, in a single step the user is able to find the main reasons for the drop from various levels of the cube. The same discovery would take longer if done manually using the available drill-down and roll-up operations.
