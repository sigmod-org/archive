The user starts with zero knowledge about the cube. Instead of exploring this data on his own, he can use the inform operator to guide his search. He starts by first invoking the "Informative View" macro that returns the most informative views of the cube in the neighborhood of the current view. Starting from the top-level view, shown as the first screen-shot in the adjacent figure he can ask for the most informative values to drill-down further. From the current view there are five drill-down paths along each of the five dimensions. Of these the most informative view returned to him is the Gender dimension. This shows that the fraction of males is 0.9 which is significantly higher than the average value of 0.5. The next surprising path is along the Category dimension which shows that the fraction of students in each category is also highly skewed. The members are sorted based on their information content. The most informative member is the Indian category that shows that the total number of Indians is 8223 --- that is 82\% of the total of 10083. This significantly departs from the expectation of having equal number of students in each of the nine categories. This cell is informative not only because it tells us that the fraction of Indians is high but also because it brings us closer to the actual values of the other members. When coupled with the knowledge in the first view that the sum of all values is 10083, we expect all other members to have a value of (10083-8223)/8 = 223 which is closer to their actual values. The next most informative dimension is the ProgCat dimension followed by the Year and finally the Dept dimension.
