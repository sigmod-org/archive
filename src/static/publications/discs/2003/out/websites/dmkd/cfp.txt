Workshop Scope & Objectives

The Workshop on Research Issues on Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery (DMKD)
was started six years ago in an effort to foster discussion and exploration
of data-mining research issues pertinent to large databases and data warehouses.
During these years, the data-mining research community has evolved and grown at
an unprecedented rate, and the area has matured significantly as a  scientific
discipline. 

This year's seventh DMKD workshop aims to bring together data-mining researchers 
and experienced practitioners with the goal of discussing the next generation of
data-mining tools.  The workshop will foster an informal atmosphere, where 
researchers and practitioners can freely interact through short presentations 
and open discussions on their ongoing work, as well as forward-looking research
visions/experiences on the next generation of  data-mining applications. 

In addition to research on novel data-mining algorithms and experiences with 
innovative mining systems, of particular interest in this year's DMKD workshop
is the broad theme of "Data Mining in Next-Generation Systems and Applications 
Management".   Topics of interest include (but are certainly not limited to):

 * Mining Techniques for Semi/Un-Structured Databases and the World-Wide Web
 * Continuous Data Mining over Streaming Data
 * Knowledge Discovery in Biological Databases and Bioinformatics
 * Data Mining Applications in Network Management
 * Mining Tools for Program Analysis and Software Engineering
 * Industrial Experiences with Data Mining Tools
 * Research Visions: What are the "killer apps" of the future?

Submission Guidelines

Submitted papers should not exceed 6-8 pages. The workshop accepts only electronic
submission of papers in ASCII, HTML, PDF, or PostScript format to either of the two
workshop chairs (vganti@microsoft.com or minos@bell-labs.com). Accepted papers will
be included in the informal proceedings made available to the workshop attendees and 
on-line through the workshop's web page.
